The three STARS Research credits do not apply to Okanagan College. Two of them apply only where research counts in academic staff promotion or tenure decisions, and the third only where an institution hosts four or more organized research centres. That takes 23 points out of the denominator rather than scoring them at zero, which makes every rung of the rating ladder easier to reach. The impact area stays in the plan because of what it produces for the other twelve. The indicators below are the College's own; none of them earns a point here.

Where this stands today

Okanagan College already does sustainability research. Five of its seven published applied research projects address a sustainability challenge, and a Sustainable Practices concentration has been part of its research strategy since 2023. What is missing is the College itself as the subject: no published project studies an Okanagan College campus or operation, and the campus work that does happen runs outside the research function, uncounted.

How to read these figures. Every project figure on this page is a reading of the College's public applied research pages, not of its project files. A full count, with a sustainability flag and a campus flag on each project, is the first activity in Year 1.

Applied Research this brief

The situation

Okanagan College runs an established applied research function. It has six staffed positions, a research ethics board, a published integrity policy, a data management strategy, five internal funding instruments and three federal funders, all set out in a 2023 strategy shaped by 67 employees and 46 students. The Sustainability Plan Framework leaves this area's baseline blank, and that reflects a question not yet asked rather than an absence of activity.

The research is already pointed at sustainability. The research centre at the Penticton campus works on water sustainability, wastewater remediation and by-product upcycling for the beverage sector. Other published projects cover winery and brewery waste, plastics on Okanagan and Skaha lake beaches, Indigenous-informed leadership training and a shoulder season tourism study. Its partner page names climate change and water use as priorities.

Campus work exists too, but it sits elsewhere. A student waste audit completed in March 2026 is the only baseline in the Supply Chain area, and three operational challenges went to a third-year business course in Fall 2026. Neither appears in any applied research record. The College's open access repository, opened in spring 2026, now accepts reports, student capstone work and retroactive deposits.

The College has also built research capabilities STARS would reward if the credits applied: a published ethical code of conduct and repository hosting.

Where the peers stand

The two Gold universities score research because it counts toward promotion there. The University of the Fraser Valley earns 19.47 of 23 Research points and the University of Northern British Columbia 16.39, their published figures. Both reach 100 per cent on Applied Learning, which the College reaches through Curriculum.

Campus-as-subject research is a university pattern so far. UBC Okanagan runs a yearly Campus as a Living Lab call, and the British Columbia Institute of Technology has run living labs since 2007. No college in the peer set does, which leaves room for Okanagan College to lead.

The closest college-scale tool is a small grant with a deposit rule. Thompson Rivers University offers two $5,000 faculty awards a year and requires each final report in its open access archive.

The applied research case

The chain this area has to complete runs from a campus problem to a changed College practice: a problem drawn from the plan is posted where researchers and instructors already look for partners, a student or researcher project takes it on, the output is deposited, and a College decision cites it. The first two links exist. The last three have not yet happened on a College campus.

  2. ExistsA route partners already use
  3. Not yet on campusStudent or researcher project
  4. Not yet on campusOutput deposited
  5. Not yet on campusCollege decision cites it

The gap is the subject, not the topic.

Five of seven published projects already address sustainability. Every one of them studies another organization's operations. Closing the gap means pointing existing capacity at the College's own campuses, not building a new research program.

The work was uncounted because it had nowhere to go.

The waste audit and the Fall 2026 course challenges produced real evidence about College operations. The repository now gives that evidence a permanent public home, so the remaining step is a habit of depositing, which costs far less than infrastructure.

With no points available, the case is operational.

This area cannot be defended on a STARS score. It earns its place by producing evidence, measurement capacity and student labour for the other twelve areas, and that case survives any future change to the manual.

Where carbon comes in. Carbon appears here only where a project studies campus energy, water or waste. When it does, the tonnes belong to Infrastructure, Supply Chain or Carbon Zero, which count them; this area supplies the evidence. No activity below carries a scope tag.

The rest of the picture

Research support is concentrated in Kelowna.

Five of six positions are at Kelowna and the sixth is a Vernon secondment. The research centre is at Penticton and Salmon Arm has no listed presence. A four-campus living lab has to plan for that spread.

The strategy carries no targets.

The 2023 strategy names commitments without numbers, dates or a review cycle, apart from four partner sessions, four student sessions and an annual expo each year. Those are the only countable commitments the College has made in this domain.

Deposits are permanent.

Repository items cannot be changed or easily withdrawn. Working drafts would build a public chain of superseded versions, and some plan evidence, such as campus safety assessments, should never be deposited. A screen comes before the first deposit.

The capability is real even where the credit is closed.

Read against the manual, the College would meet the ethical code indicator and one of four open access routes today. The recognition indicator needs promotion guidelines and cannot be met. About 1.7 of 7 points would be in reach, not the 6 earlier estimated.

How this was worked out

The tier axis was tested rather than inherited. Five candidates were considered: dependence on outside money, dependence on outside parties, how far the College commits beyond what is required, campus coverage, and how far the College acts as a client of its own applied research.

The last one separates three real options and is adopted. Outside money separates only the top option, because the first two run on instruments the College already has. Outside parties applies to every option equally. Commitment beyond requirement has nothing to measure against, because no credit applies. Campus coverage is part of the outcome target and separates two options, not three.

Every College figure is taken from a named source or left blank. No cost is estimated; where none exists the entry reads not yet costed.

AC 6 is worth 10 points: 8 for the share of research-engaged departments doing sustainability research and 2 for incentives. AC 7 is worth 6 for an organized sustainability research unit, or 3 for a unit focused elsewhere that produces sustainability research. AC 8 is worth 7: 1 for a published ethical code of conduct, 3 for promotion guidelines that recognize integrated, community-based and extra-academic research, 1 for an inter-campus responsible research network and 2 for open access support. The three add to 23, as the manual states.

Two AC 8 tables add to more than their stated maxima. Indicator 8.2 lists six items at 0.75, a total of 4.5 against up to 3, and indicator 8.4 lists five items totalling 2.67 against up to 2. Each is resolved by its own rule (two of three criteria, three of four ways), the same pattern as EN 9, and joins the plan's single written inquiry to AASHE.

AC 4 has no gate. Criterion A must be met before B, C or D count. Criterion D asks for an online portal or equivalent that documents completed, current or prospective applied learning projects; the repository can carry the completed projects, and Curriculum scopes the rest.

Neither the manual nor the Innovation and Leadership catalogue states an applicability gate for the Research bonus credits. The research inventory credit therefore appears open to the College, which keeps the UNSDGs indicator on its 0 to 4 scale. Confirmation from AASHE is an open question.

The target, stated twice

Outcome target

By the end of FY2031/32 every one of the four campuses has hosted a completed applied research or student living lab project that takes the campus or its operations as its subject, at least one such project completes each year from FY2027/28, and at least two findings have been adopted into a College operating decision.

Baseline: 0 of 7 published projects study a College campus or operation (public pages, October 2026). Two pieces of campus work, a March 2026 waste audit and Fall 2026 course challenges, sit outside the research record.

Why this one: it tests the second half of the goal statement directly, and it counts campuses as well as projects, so a four-campus living lab cannot be met at Kelowna alone. No college in the peer set runs a comparable program, so the numbers are set from the College's own four campuses and six-year calendar rather than a peer benchmark.

Lever target

Supply the evidence Curriculum needs to claim AC 4 Applied Learning at 4 of 4 in November 2028, and the research inventory UNSDGs needs for its bonus credit in the same submission. No point is counted in this area.

Baseline: AC 4 not yet claimed; no applied learning portal, partner feedback method or research inventory exists.

Why this one: no Research credit applies, so the only STARS-scored units this area moves are the ones it feeds. Scoring them externally keeps the lever honest, and counting them once keeps the ledger clean.

Neither target is a forecast. No third card is used: the framework records no baseline that the two cards leave uncovered.

What the tags and timing mean

Measure produces or improves a number. Enable makes delivery possible: a decision, a route, a screen or a funding theme. Deliver moves the area's own outcome: a campus project, a deposit, an adopted finding. Timing bands: Immediate, within months; Short, by 2028; Medium, by 2030; Long, by 2032.

The decisions we need

Should the College add two flags to the project records it already keeps?

A yes or no for sustainability and a yes or no for a College campus or operation as subject, with no new inventory and no dollar values. It is the cheapest route to a real Year 1 count, and it turns this brief's central finding from a reading of public pages into a measured fact. Recommendation: yes, from FY2026/27.

Should internal units post campus problems through the existing partner route?

If the partner intake route can take internal problems with a line of guidance, the gap closes through an administrative change rather than a new program. If it cannot, a route has to be designed, which is a resourcing conversation. Recommendation: use the existing route and confirm it first.

Who screens a proposed deposit for confidential or privacy-sensitive content?

Repository administrators check eligibility, scope and format, not content, and deposits are effectively permanent. Some plan evidence, such as campus safety assessments, must never be deposited. Recommendation: the Sustainability Office screens plan material before deposit, and only stable milestone versions go in.

Should the College pursue green laboratory certification at its research centre?

The Green Laboratory bonus credit pays 0.5 points for one certified laboratory and 1 for half of all laboratories, with no applicability gate. It is the one point this area could earn directly, but the cost is not yet known. Recommendation: decide in FY2028/29 after costing, and count it here if adopted.

Which of the three options should the College adopt?

Record and route costs least but leaves campus projects to chance. Funding research on itself commits money before the College knows what campus projects deliver. Recommendation: post the College's own problems, and revisit a dedicated fund at the FY2029/30 review.
